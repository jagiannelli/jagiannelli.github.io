    Section 9.2 Area Under a Curve

    A definite integral \(\displaystyle\int_a^b y\,dx\) is the area between the curve and the \(x\)-axis from \(x = a\) to \(x = b\) when the curve is above the axis. Where the curve is below the axis, \(y\) is negative, so the integral is negative: it is minus the area. An area is never negative. So when the curve crosses the axis between \(a\) and \(b\text{,}\) split at the roots, do each region on its own, and add the areas. The cells share their variables, so run them in order from the top.
    🔗

    Subsection 9.2.1 Roots, regions, one integral each

    Take \(y = x(x - 2)(x - 5)\text{.}\) First the setup, and the curve expanded ready to integrate:
    🔗
    Step 1, the roots. The curve crosses the \(x\)-axis where \(y = 0\text{.}\) solve finds them (here they can also be read off the factors). They are the limits of the regions.
    🔗
    Step 2, a picture. plot(..., fill='axis') shades between the curve and the axis. Blue is above the axis and red below, so you can see which integral will be negative: the curve is above the axis from \(0\) to \(2\) and below it from \(2\) to \(5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 3, one integral per region. integrate(f, x, a, b) gives \(\int_a^b f\,dx\text{.}\) The blue region is above the axis, so its integral is its area. The red region is below, so its integral is minus its area.
    🔗
    Step 4, the total area. The area of each region is the size of its integral, abs: the blue one as it is, the red one with its minus sign taken off. Add the two.
    🔗

    Subsection 9.2.2 Why one integral across the axis is wrong

    From \(0\) to \(5\) in one go, the blue area (positive) and the red area (negative) partly cancel, and the result is not an area at all. It is the two integrals added with their signs, which is not what was asked:
    🔗
    Swapping the limits changes the sign of a definite integral, so the smaller \(x\) goes at the bottom: from the left-hand root to the next one. Taken the wrong way round, the first region gives minus its area, which then looks like a region below the axis:

    Subsection 9.2.3 Practice with random questions

    Each seed gives a new cubic in factors, \(y = \pm(x - p)(x - q)(x - r)\text{,}\) with three different whole-number roots, so part of the curve is above the axis and part below. The same seed always gives the same question. Sketch it and work it on paper first, then type the total area as a number: a fraction like 253/12, or a decimal within 0.01 of it. Do not type a minus sign: an area is never negative. The checker says whether it is right and, if not, what the usual slip looks like.
