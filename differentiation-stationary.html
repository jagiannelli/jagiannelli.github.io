    Section 8.3 Stationary Points and Curve Sketching

    A stationary point of a curve is a point where the gradient is zero, \(f'(x) = 0\text{:}\) the tangent is flat. Most stationary points are the top of a hill (a maximum) or the bottom of a valley (a minimum), and finding them is how you locate the highest and lowest values of a function. Four steps do the job:
    🔗
    1. differentiate, twice: \(f'(x)\) and \(f''(x)\text{;}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. solve \(f'(x) = 0\) for the \(x\) values;
      🔗
      🔗
    3. put each \(x\) into \(f(x)\) for its \(y\text{;}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. decide the nature from \(f''\) at that \(x\text{:}\) \(f''(x) \gt 0\) is a minimum, \(f''(x) \lt 0\) is a maximum.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    The second derivative is only a tool here, used to tell a maximum from a minimum. The cells share their variables, so run them in order from the top.
    🔗

    Subsection 8.3.1 Finding the stationary points

    Take \(f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 12x + 5\text{.}\) diff(f, x) differentiates once and diff(f, x, 2) twice. First the setup, then step 1:
    🔗
    Step 2. factor shows the working you would write, and solve gives the \(x\) values. It answers with equations like x == 2; .rhs() keeps the right-hand side, the number, and sorted puts them in order.
    🔗
    Steps 3 and 4. For each \(x\text{,}\) find \(y = f(x)\) and the sign of \(f''(x)\text{:}\)
    🔗
    The same, one step per line, the way it is written on paper:
    🔗
    Habits that keep this right: write \(f'(x) = 0\) down before solving; keep every term of \(f'(x)\text{,}\) the constant included (\(-12x\) differentiates to \(-12\text{,}\) not to nothing); give both coordinates of each point; and say what the sign of \(f''\) means.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 8.3.2 f, f′ and f″ side by side

    The black dots are the stationary points. At the maximum, \(f'\) crosses zero going down (the gradient goes from positive to negative) and \(f''\) is negative. At the minimum, \(f'\) crosses zero going up and \(f''\) is positive.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 8.3.3 When f″ is zero

    If \(f''(x) = 0\) at a stationary point the test says nothing. \(y = x^4\) (a minimum), \(y = -x^4\) (a maximum) and \(y = x^3\) (neither) all have \(f''(0) = 0\text{.}\) Look instead at the sign of \(f'\) just either side: negative then positive is a minimum, positive then negative is a maximum, and the same sign on both sides is neither (a point of inflection).
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 8.3.4 Sketching a cubic

    A sketch needs the right shape, the points where the curve crosses the axes (with their coordinates), and the stationary points on the correct side of each axis. It need not be to scale. Find four things first:
    🔗
    1. the shape, from the sign of \(x^3\text{:}\) a positive cubic comes up from the bottom left and leaves at the top right, so its maximum comes before its minimum; a negative cubic is the other way round, with the minimum first;
      🔗
      🔗
    2. the \(y\)-axis: put \(x = 0\text{;}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. the \(x\)-axis: solve \(f(x) = 0\text{,}\) by the factor theorem;
      🔗
      🔗
    4. the stationary points, as before, and which quadrant each is in.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Two examples: the positive cubic \(y = (x + 3)(x - 1)(x - 2) = x^3 - 7x + 6\) and the negative cubic \(y = -(x + 4)(x - 1)(x - 3) = -x^3 + 13x - 12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The crossings are in green and the stationary points in black. In the first the maximum comes first; in the second the minimum does.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 8.3.5 When the roots are surds

    Not every \(f'(x) = 0\) factorises. For \(g(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 3x + 2\text{,}\) \(g'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x - 3\text{,}\) and \(x^2 - 2x - 1 = 0\) needs completing the square (or the formula). Leave the answers exact, with the decimal beside them.

    Subsection 8.3.6 Largest and smallest values

    A real problem is the same method on a formula you build first: write the quantity in terms of one letter, differentiate, set the derivative to 0, and show that the value is a maximum (or minimum). Say a box with no lid is made from a 30 cm square of card by cutting a square of side \(x\) cm from each corner and folding up the sides. The box is \(x\) tall with a square base of side \(30 - 2x\text{,}\) so its volume is \(V = x(30 - 2x)^2\text{,}\) with \(0 \lt x \lt 15\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The volume is small for a shallow box (small \(x\)) and for a tall, thin one (\(x\) near 15), and largest in between: 2000 cm\(^3\) when the cut-out squares are 5 cm.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 8.3.7 Practice with random questions

    Each seed gives a cubic \(f(x) = ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d\text{,}\) positive or negative, built from its two stationary \(x\) values so they come out exact. About one question in four has a stationary point at a third. The same seed always gives the same question.
