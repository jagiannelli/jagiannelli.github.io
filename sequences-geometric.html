    Section 5.2 Geometric Sequences and Series

    A geometric sequence is a list of numbers in which each term is a fixed multiple of the preceding one. If the first term is \(a\) and the common ratio is \(r\text{,}\) the sequence is
    \begin{equation*} a,\; ar,\; ar^2,\; ar^3,\; \dots \end{equation*}
    and the \(n\)th term is
    \begin{equation*} u_n = ar^{n-1}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The sum of the first \(n\) terms is
    \begin{equation*} S_n = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}, \quad r \neq 1. \end{equation*}
    When \(|r| \lt 1\text{,}\) the term \(r^n \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\text{,}\) and the series converges to the sum to infinity:
    \begin{equation*} S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r}, \quad |r| \lt 1. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    This section investigates geometric sequences and convergence in Sage, builds an interactive slider showing partial sums approaching \(S_\infty\text{,}\) and provides diagnostic practice questions. The cells share their variables, so run them in order from the top.
    🔗

    Subsection 5.2.2 Convergence and the Sum to Infinity

    When \(|r| \lt 1\text{,}\) the terms shrink towards zero. The partial sums \(S_1, S_2, S_3, \dots\) approach the limit \(S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r}\text{.}\) When \(|r| \geq 1\text{,}\) the terms do not shrink and the series diverges. Sage can compute partial sums and show the convergence graphically:
    🔗
    A key application of \(S_\infty\) is converting recurring decimals to fractions. For instance, \(0.\\overline{36} = 0.363636\dots\) is a geometric series with first term \(a = 0.36\) and ratio \(r = 0.01\text{:}\)
